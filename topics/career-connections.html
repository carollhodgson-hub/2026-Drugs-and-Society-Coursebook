Featured degree connection

Human Services Technology: Substance Abuse and Recovery Studies

SOC-245 Drugs and Society is a required course for this degree pathway. The course helps future human-services professionals understand substance use as biological behavior, lived experience, social identity, public-health concern, family issue, economic force, and policy question—not simply as an individual moral failing.

Knowledge for practice

Drug classifications, effects, interactions, dependence, treatment, recovery, harm reduction, prevention, law, stigma, and social inequality.

Professional skills

Nonjudgmental communication, evidence evaluation, cultural humility, ethical reasoning, crisis awareness, resource navigation, and systems thinking.

Possible settings

Treatment and recovery programs, community agencies, peer-support services, prevention programs, shelters, courts, healthcare systems, outreach programs, and public health.

Course completion alone does not confer a professional license or credential. Job titles, education, supervision, certification, and licensure requirements vary by employer and jurisdiction.

Transferable skills employers can see

Communicate without stigma

Use precise, person-centered language with clients, families, coworkers, and communities.

Evaluate evidence

Distinguish data, interpretation, correlation, causation, claims, and uncertainty.

Think across systems

Connect individual experience with families, culture, healthcare, law, education, and the economy.

Act ethically

Recognize privacy, consent, unequal power, professional limits, and the need for referral.
