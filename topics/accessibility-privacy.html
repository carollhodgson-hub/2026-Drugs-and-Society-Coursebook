Accessibility, ADA Support, and Privacy

This open coursebook is designed to reduce barriers while recognizing that accessibility is an ongoing process shaped by individual needs, devices, browsers, and assistive technologies.

Accessibility statement

This coursebook was created with the goal of meeting Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 Level AA. Beyond the Substance: Understanding Drugs in Culture & Society is designed with accessibility in mind and aims to support the principles of the Americans with Disabilities Act and the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines. Features include semantic headings, descriptive links, alternative text for meaningful images, captions or transcripts when available, keyboard-operable controls, visible focus indicators, color contrast, responsive layouts, reduced-motion support, and written explanations accompanying interactive material.

No student should be required to rely on color, sound, animation, dragging, or an interactive graphic alone to obtain essential course content. The surrounding text, tables, captions, or alternative instructions are intended to provide an equivalent route to the central learning objective.

Keyboard and screen readers

Navigation and custom controls are designed for keyboard use and labeled for assistive technology. Standard browser zoom and text enlargement should remain usable.

Images and charts

Instructional images include alternative text or nearby explanations. Complex charts are paired with summaries or data tables when the exact relationships matter.

Audio and video

Embedded media should use captions or transcripts when the provider makes them available. A direct link is supplied when an embedded player may be inaccessible.

Interactives

Interactive elements reinforce learning; they do not replace all written instruction. Students may use the text-based explanation when an activity is inaccessible or technically unavailable.

Privacy statement

The coursebook does not ask students to create an account or enter names, student identification numbers, diagnoses, medical histories, substance-use histories, or other sensitive personal information. Interactive calculations and selections are intended for learning and operate within the student’s browser; they are not designed to submit answers to the author or create a student record.

  • Do not enter identifiable information. Examples and calculators should use hypothetical information, not private medical, legal, or employment details.
  • Canvas remains the official course system. Grades, assignments, accommodations, and instructor communication belong in the college-approved systems identified by the instructor.
  • External media have separate policies. YouTube and other linked resources may receive ordinary technical information and may use cookies or account settings under their own privacy policies.
  • Normal hosting records may exist. As with most websites, the hosting service may process technical information needed to deliver and secure the pages, such as an IP address, browser type, requested page, and time of access.
  • This is education, not personal assessment. A calculator, quiz, chart, or example does not diagnose a condition, establish impairment, determine legal responsibility, or replace medical, legal, counseling, or emergency services.

Requesting access or reporting a barrier

If any part of the coursebook is difficult to perceive, navigate, understand, or operate, students should contact the instructor and use the college’s established accessibility or disability-services process. An alternative format or equivalent activity can be provided without requiring a student to disclose private medical details in a public forum.

Please report: the section number, the name of the image or activity, the device or assistive technology being used if comfortable sharing it, and what prevented access. Do not include diagnoses or sensitive health information in a general technical report.

Accessibility feedback should be treated as part of continuing course improvement, not as a student failure. Because external videos, laws, links, and browser behavior can change, this statement describes the design goal rather than guaranteeing that every third-party resource will work perfectly in every environment.

Student choice and sensitive content

This course discusses addiction, overdose, suicide, sexual assault, trafficking, violence, pregnancy, illness, stigma, and death. These topics are academically necessary but may connect to personal experiences. Students are encouraged to use available support, communicate privately with the instructor about access needs, and follow Canvas directions for any approved alternative. Students should never be asked to reveal personal drug use, recovery status, trauma history, disability, or family experience to complete a public activity.
